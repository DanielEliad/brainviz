Matan Haver 1 a, Sjir J.C. Schielen 1 b, Danny Ruijters 1 , 2 c, Albert P. Aldenkamp 1 , 3 d and Svitlana
Zinger 1 e
1 Department of Electrical Engineering, Eindhoven University of Technology, The Netherlands
2 Image Guided Therapy, Philips Healthcare
3 Department of Behavioral Sciences, Epilepsy Center Kempenhaeghe
Keywords: Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging, Resting-State Networks, Functional Connectivity, ABIDE, ASD
Abstract: Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) diagnosis currently depends on subjective behavioral assessments, motivating the search for objective neuroimaging biomarkers. This study evaluates different types of connectivity
measures from Resting-State Function Magnetic Resonance Imaging (rs-fMRI) to diagnose ASD in a large
multisite dataset. Different Functional Connectivity (FC) measures were computed between the time series
corresponding to resting state networks. FC was obtained via the Pearson and Spearman Correlation Coefficient (PCC and SCC), but also through a non-linear lag-independent method called Dynamic Time Warping
(DTW). Feature vectors were input to five different classifiers, obtaining their performance matrices, which
were assessed using nested cross-validation to prevent data leakage. The highest classification accuracy (65%)
was achieved using DTW features on a homogeneous sub dataset, indicating that non-linear correlation may
outperform linear methods under consistent scan protocols. Compared to prior studies that often rely solely on
linear correlation methods and use relatively small datasets, this research highlights the importance of robust
validation, non-linear metrics and consistent scanning protocols in developing reliable ASD classifiers from
rs-fMRI.
**1 INTRODUCTION**
Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by deficits in
social interaction, communication, and restrictive,
repetitive patterns of behavior. Early diagnosis of
this condition is essential to enable timely intervention and effective treatment. Currently, ASD identification often involves behavioral observation, which in
some cases can be prolonged and may require the expertise of a multidisciplinary team of highly trained
specialists. However, in some cases, limited understanding of the underlying neuropathology may lead
to suboptimal treatment outcomes, as the current diagnosis remains subjective while an objective one is
desired [S´olon et al., 2017] [Insel et al., 2010].
Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI),
an imaging technique that measures brain activity,

has gained significant popularity in recent decades
for investigating potential differences between individuals with ASD and Healthy Controls (HC) [Santana et al., 2022] [Schielen et al., 2024b]. Its ability
to monitor brain activity with sufficiently high spatial resolution in a safe and noninvasive manner plays
a key rule driving its widespread adoption [Poldrack
et al., 2011]. A variety of studies seek to identify a
neural biomarker for ASD by measuring the byproduct of neural activity, the concentration of deoxygenated hemoglobin known as the Blood-OxygenLevel-Dependent (BOLD) signal.
Several approaches aim to identify such a
biomarker, commonly through Functional Connectivity (FC). FC is a connectivity method which describes
the statistical dependency between the activity of two
brain regions.
FC can be assessed using various methods, with
Pearson’s Correlation Coefficient (PCC) being the
most commonly used method in diagnostic MRI research [Santana et al., 2022] [Schielen et al., 2024b].
The PCC method assumes synchronization and measures the linear relationship between two time series
but does not account for time lags or variations in
speed between them. In addition, PCC assumes widesense stationarity, meaning that the mean and the covariance between the input signals remain unchanged
over the course of the experiment. The stationarity of fMRI signals can be challenged, especially in
the common event of head motion [Laumann et al.,
2017]. Another method examined is Spearman’s Correlation Coefficient (SCC), a rank-based correlation
method that does not assume stationarity or linearity,
allowing it to identify linear and non-linear correlations between time series whose statistical properties
can vary with time. SCC captures monotonic trends,
but it does not necessarily account for temporal misalignments.
In contrast, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is
a non-linear correlation method, which estimates
the cost of warping one signal to align with another and therefore accommodates temporal misalignments, shifts, and non-stationary signals. The latter
deserves closer attention as the assumption of stationarity is often not satisfied in fMRI [Muhei-Aldin et al.,
2014]. This motivates the relatively novel computations of DC through DTW.
This research aims to compare the use of the PCC,
SCC, and DTW methods to evaluate which method
more efficiently improves ASD classification, using
the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange (ABIDE)
dataset [Martino, 2017] [Martino, 2014] [Schielen
et al., 2024a].
This paper makes three key contributions: first, it
evaluates the use of DTW as a non-linear method for
FC, challenging the commonly used PCC for FC. It
is also compared to SCC, which is similar to PCC
but does not assume linearity. Second, it examines
the impact of dataset heterogeneity on classification
performance by comparing results across homogeneous and heterogeneous data subsets, and demonstrates that DTW outperforms other correlation methods in a large homogeneous subset. Lastly, it offers a
potential framework for the clinical interpretation of
the classification results.
The remainder of the paper starts with a state-ofthe-art review in section 2, followed by the methodology applied in this research in Section 3. The results
are discussed in Section 4, with the drawn conclusions
provided in Section 5.
**2 RELATED WORK**
In this section, the current literature approaches to
classifying ASD with fMRI are discussed. FC is expressed between the time series of two regions of interest (ROIs). Two ways of obtaining these exist: a
predefined atlas and a parcellation directly obtained
on the studied sample with group ICA. The use of
Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to identify
ROIs and remove low-frequency noise and motion effects at Resting-State fMRI (rs-fMRI) has been successfully demonstrated and applied by different researchers [Beckmann et al., 2005, Beckmann et al.,
2009,Schielen et al., 2024a]. To express statistical relationships between ROIs, various studies have relied
almost entirely on PCC, assuming only synchronized
BOLD signals indicate FC between two regions. Over
the past decade, various studies have attempted to
classify based on this FC using traditional machine
learning classifiers [Yang et al., 2019, Yang et al.,
2020, Biswal et al., 1995]. The focus is on the methods of interest to this paper - PCC, SCC, and DTW.
A 2019 study reported that a Ridge classifier
achieved an accuracy of 71.98% using a 5-fold crossvalidation and the Craddock 400 (CC400) brain atlas [Yang et al., 2019]. Cao et al. (2021) [Cao et al.,
2021] have used PCC to extract FC with a graph convolutional network classifier and a 10-fold cross validation, resulting in accuracy of 73.3%. However, the
listed studies applied hyperparameter tuning including the test set, which may cause data leakage and
lead to overoptimistic results [Rosenblatt et al., 2024].
A research from 2020 employing a deep neural network for classification attained a higher accuracy of
75.27% with a 5-fold cross-validation [Yang et al.,
2020]. More recently, Li et al. [Li et al., 2022] (2022)
suggested that a transformer-based model achieved a
classification accuracy of 74.18% with 10-fold crossvalidation.
The aforementioned studies used PCC to assess
FC with the ABIDE dataset, including more than
1000 scans of ASD and HC [Santana et al., 2022]
[Biswal et al., 1995]. PCC accounts only for the linear
correlation of different time series, which may prevent reliable identification of time series that share a
non-linear correlation pattern. Therefore, other correlation methods may outperform PCC. For instance, a
study from 2022 suggests that SCC (a non-linear correlation method) may be more applicable that PCC
[Yang et al., 2022]. Furthermore, brain atlases typically contain many ROIs which scales quadratically
with the number of features in pairwise connectivity.
This might convolute direct clinical interpretation.
A different method is DTW, a powerful non-linear
approach that compares the similarity of time series
with varying speeds. Linke et al. [Linke et al., 2020]
state that using the non-linear DTW method has detected more FC differences and improved the estimation of resting-state FC in ASD. However, the study
utilized a relatively small sample size (fewer than 200

participants) divided into three distinct age groups.
Consequently, generalizability remains to be tested on

In summary, many recent studies have delivered
promising results using FC. These studies attempted
to diagnose ASD with different methodological approaches to feature extraction techniques and classification. While the effects of the feature extraction
techniques and the choice of classifier vary across
studies, all the aforementioned researchers agree on
testing different algorithms and evaluating their performance on accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity
through k-fold cross-validation [Rosenblatt et al.,
2024]. Important to note is that FC was based on
PCC in all these studies, which involves adopting its
underlying assumptions. It remains unclear whether
considering only linear correlation misses important
information about coactivation patterns between time
series.
**3 METHODOLOGY**
To predict ASD, the suggested approach is shown in
Fig. 1. The system takes as an input the ICA time
series (14 signals) and provides a binary prediction as
an output.
**3.1** **Data Set**
A preprocessed version of the ABIDE dataset was
used in this study and comprises 900 participants: 417
subjects are diagnosed with ASD (361 males and 56
females; mean age 22 years) and 483 subjects are diagnosed as HC (377 males and 106 females; mean
age 27 years) [Schielen et al., 2024a]. A subset of
the data, consisting of 260 participants originating entirely from NYU Langone Health system, is examined. The subset represents the largest dataset pro
viding a homogeneous scanning protocol, potentially
leading to improved classification performance and
reliability.
Initially, the rs-fMRI data were preprocessed to
ensure slice-timing alignment, spatial normalization,
smoothing and noise reduction. Then, a group ICA
was performed on the fMRI data, where the components corresponding to the RSNs were selected with
the Smith atlas as an initial reference [Beckmann
et al., 2009]. This procedure yielded spatial maps,
which then served as a design matrix in a multiple
linear regression to derive subject-specific time series [Nickerson et al., 2017]. Each time series is constructed from 146 samples, as illustrated in Fig. 2.
The study’s results are focused on 14 of the 32
identified independent components, examining their
inter-network correlation patterns to improve ASD
classification and to explore their potential clinical
significance. The 14 components and their labeling
are shown in Fig. 3.

considered to characterize brain connectivity patterns.
The motivation behind the choice of extracting the
PCC, SCC, and DTW features stems from considering the implicit assumption of linearity in PCC and
investigating if a non-linear method can outperform.

**3.3** **Feature Selection**
The applied feature-selection strategy is a forward sequential selection. This feature selection algorithm
provides the most informative subset of features for
the model. The process is initialized with the single
most dominant feature and iteratively adds the next
most contributive feature until 10 features have been
selected. At each iteration, given a subset of selected
features, the next additional feature is chosen so that
the new set achieves the best performance [Kamalov
et al., 2024]. This approach was chosen to find a set of
strongly correlated features that can support a clinical
interpretation for ASD classification.
**3.4** **Classification**
The features of each participant were labeled with
ASD or HC to allow supervised learning. Several machine learning algorithms were applied to identify the
most effective classifier for the feature space, including Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF),
Support Vector Machine (SVM), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) and Neural Network (NN). The classifiers
were trained on the features data and then assessed.
To achieve a more reliable and generalized model,
a 5-fold cross validation was applied. This method divides the data into random 5 sub-datasets, where for
each training iteration one subset is used as the test
set and the remaining subsets are used as the training set. Within each fold, an inner 3-fold CV guides
feature addition and prevents data leakage by ensuring each subset is evaluated on unseen data. The

**4.2** **Feature Selection**
A sequential feature selection algorithm was applied, using SequentialFeatureSelector from the
scikit-learn Python library.
Since both aforementioned metrics yield feature
vectors in which each index corresponds to the same
pair of time series, the frequency of selection for
each feature was recorded to identify the most consistently chosen features. A selection-frequency analysis across all CV folds and classifier types identified the most recurrent features. Table 2 shows the
10 time-series pairs that exhibited the greatest consistency, along their recurrence frequency. Each selected
pair of networks correlation serves as a basis for ASD
classification. The connectivity between the anterior
and posterior default mode networks (DMNs) proved
to be the most informative, selected in 23 out of 50
folds. This was followed by distinguished correlations involving the salience and language networks,
the sensorimotor and frontoparietal networks, and the
frontoparietal and visual sub networks (see Fig. 6).
Related studies have reported that individuals with
ASD often experience communication difficulties,
which are associated with decreased FC between the
anterior and posterior DMNs, supporting the findings
of the present research (see Fig. 4 [Padmanabhan
et al., 2017, Di Martino et al., 2014]. FC between
the salience and language networks, identified in this
study as highly informative, has likewise been reported to show decreased connectivity in ASD, further reinforcing the results [Bernas, 2020]. Moreover,
decreased connectivity between the lateral sensorimotor and right frontoparietal networks has also been
documented in the literature, in agreement with the
findings [Minnigulova et al., 2025].

**4.3** **Classification**
The classification was performed using the Python library scikit-learn. Across all metrics, the applied classifiers shared the same configurations. LR was set
with a maximum of 1000 iterations, while the SVM
used a linear kernel. The RF was assembled of 100
decision trees, KNN included 5 neighbors and the
neural networks consisted of a single hidden layer
with 100 perceptrons, a maximum of 1000 iterations
and a learning rate of 0.005. Table 1 represents the
assessment of the examined classifiers. Initial classification was performed using all examined metrics.
Among them, SCC achieved the highest classification
accuracy of 63% (AUC = 0.62) using an SVM classifier, followed by PCC with an accuracy of 61% (AUC
= 0.61). DTW features achieved the best result with
an SVM classifier reaching an accuracy of 58% (AUC
= 0.57). Based on these results, it can be concluded
that ASD classification using the linear monotonic relationships (SCC) of all examined RSNs across the
entire dataset outperformed the other observed metrics.
After evaluating the classification performance using all features, the performance based on the selected
features was subsequently examined. The highest performance was achieved using PCC features with a Logistic Regression classifier, reaching an accuracy of
59% (AUC = 0.58). SCC and DTW-based classifications yielded lower accuracies, with maximum values
of 58% and 57%, respectively. Applying feature selection to the entire dataset resulted in decreased clas
sification performance across all metrics. This decline
in performance suggests there is no subset of features
that sufficiently describes the heterogeneity inherent
to ASD, as also observed by [Masi et al., 2017].
Finally, classification performance based on the
selected features was evaluated using the NYU subdataset, allowing analysis on a more homogeneous
dataset. This evaluation revealed that DTW-based
features yielded the highest performance, achieving
an accuracy of 65% (AUC = 0.67) with a LR classifier (see Fig. 5). Following that, SCC-based features
reached an accuracy of 54% (AUC = 0.54) using an
RF classifier, while PCC-based features resulted in
slightly lower performance, with an accuracy of 54%
(AUC = 0.53) using a NN classifier. This result suggests that non-linear connectivity, when using homogeneous fMRI scans, can outperform linear connectivity in classification.
**5 CONCLUSIONS**
Autism spectrum disorder diagnosis remains a challenging task and an objective diagnosis is desired.
This study aimed to address this challenge by evaluating multiple brain connectivity metrics - PCC, SCC,
and DTW - for classifying ASD using resting-state
fMRI scans. While modest classification performance
was achieved overall, DTW features showed better
results than the rest of the examined metrics. The
best performance (65% accuracy) was achieved using DTW-based features with a Logistic Regression

classifier on the large homogeneous NYU sub dataset
(including 260 subjects).
Feature selection did not consistently improve
performance across the full dataset, likely due to
noise and site-related variability in the data. However, selected features performed better only when
using DTW features with the homogeneous scanning
protocol NYU sub dataset, which highlights the effectiveness of non-linear correlation methods under
consistent scanning protocols. Additionally, the brain
networks identified as informative align with findings
from clinically related literature on autism diagnosis.
These findings demonstrate that non-linear correlation measures such as DTW can offer a meaningful
advantage over traditional linear methods. They also
reinforce the value of nested cross-validation and ho
mogeneous scanning protocol data sources in developing generalizable classifiers.
Nonetheless, this study has several limitations.
The method shows reduced performance when applied to heterogeneous datasets with varying scanning
protocols, which may affect its generalizability. In
addition, the calculation of DTW distances is approximated due to computational complexity, which may
lead to reduced alignment precision. Moreover, the
highest diagnostic accuracy achieved falls below the
threshold typically required for clinical application.
Future research should explore the integration of alternative brain atlases and evaluate more advanced
classification frameworks such as deep neural networks or transformers to further enhance diagnostic
accuracy.
